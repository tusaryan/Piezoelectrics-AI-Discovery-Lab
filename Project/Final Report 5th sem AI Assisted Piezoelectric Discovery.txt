ABSTRACT




The global transition toward sustainable electronics is currently hindered by the slow pace of material discovery. The industry standard for piezoelectric sensors, Lead Zirconate Titanate (PZT), contains over 60% toxic lead by weight, facing imminent bans under global regulations like RoHS. This project addresses the "Lead Dilemma" by developing Piezo.AI, a machine learning-driven platform designed to accelerate the discovery of lead-free alternatives, specifically focusing on Potassium-Sodium Niobate (KNN) solid solutions. 


A curated dataset of 400+ experimental ceramic compositions (with 250+ KNN based piezoelectric material properties) from peer-reviewed literature and utilizing domain-specific feature engineering to train advanced machine learning models—including LightGBM, XGBoost, Random Forest, SVM, and Stacked Ensembles. These models predict two key functional properties: Piezoelectric Coefficient (d33) and Curie Temperature (Tc) directly from chemical composition, reducing screening time from weeks to milliseconds. The platform is deployed as a user-friendly web application with a unique Dual-Mode Training Interface (Manual vs. Auto-Intelligent), enabling researchers to perform instant predictions, comparisons, and automated report generation.

CHAPTER 1
INTRODUCTION    






1.  Introduction
   1. Background: The Role of Piezoelectrics 

Piezoelectric materials possess the unique ability to convert mechanical stress into electrical energy (Direct Effect) and electrical energy into mechanical strain (Inverse Effect). This property makes them indispensable in modern electronics, powering devices ranging from medical ultrasound transducers and sonar systems to precision actuators in fuel injectors. 
For decades, Lead Zirconate Titanate (PZT) has been the undisputed industry standard due to its exceptional piezoelectric coefficient (d33 > 200-600 pC/N) and high Curie Temperature (Tc). Its morphotropic phase boundary (MPB) allows for easy polarization and high electromechanical coupling. 


   2. Problem Statement: The “Lead Dilemma” 

The current industry standard, Lead Zirconate Titanate (PZT), possesses excellent electromechanical properties but contains over 60% lead (Pb) by weight. Lead is a potent neurotoxin that accumulates in the environment. The disposal of PZT-based electronics leads to lead leaching into groundwater, posing severe health risks including nervous system damage in children. 
      1. Regulatory Pressure: In response, global regulatory bodies have enacted strict bans: 
I. RoHS (Restriction of Hazardous Substances): An EU directive restricting lead use in consumer electronics. 
II. REACH: Regulations on chemical substances. 
         1. The Challenge: This creates a massive demand for high-performance lead-free alternatives. However, traditional trial-and-error discovery is too slow to meet this demand.
These regulations have created an urgent need for lead-free alternatives. While materials like Potassium-Sodium Niobate (KNN), Barium Titanate (BT), and Bismuth Sodium Titanate (BNT) show promise, their pure forms often suffer from lower performance compared to PZT, requiring complex doping strategies to enhance their properties. 


         3. The Solution: Materials Informatics - A New Paradigm
 
Traditional material discovery relies on the "Edisonian" approach, trial-and-error synthesis in a laboratory. This process is: 
            1. Slow: Synthesizing, sintering, and testing a single ceramic puck takes weeks. 
            2. Expensive: Requires significant energy and raw materials. 
            3. Limited: Can only explore a tiny fraction of the infinite compositional space. 
Materials Informatics: the application of Machine Learning (ML) to this problem, offers a solution that learns the complex, non-linear relationships between chemical composition and physical properties. By training algorithms on existing data, we can create a "Virtual Laboratory" that predicts material properties in milliseconds, effectively screening thousands of candidates to identify the most promising ones for experimental validation.

CHAPTER 2
 LITERATURE REVIEW     




________________


            2.  LITREATURE REVIEW
            1. Lead-Free Alternatives: The KNN System
Research by Saito et al. demonstrated that (K,Na)NbO3 (KNN) ceramics could achieve properties comparable to PZT through texture engineering. KNN is biocompatible and has a high Curie temperature. However, optimizing KNN is difficult because:
            * It requires precise control of the K/Na ratio.
            * Volatilization of alkali elements during sintering degrades properties.
            * Dopants like Li, Ta, and Sb are needed to shift the Phase Transition temperatures to room temperature (creating a polymorphic phase boundary), but this often lowers Tc.
            * The compositional space is vast and highly non-linear, making it difficult to optimize using traditional phase diagram explorations.
            1. Existing Gaps and Limitations
While recent studies have demonstrated the efficacy of ML in materials science, existing tools suffer from:
            * Accessibility: Not user-friendly for experimentalists. They are often "black boxes" accessible only to coding experts.
            * Rigidity: Most models are static and cannot be updated with new lab data.
            * Transparency: There is limited explainability regarding model choices and hyperparameter impact. Often utilize a "one-size-fits-all" model rather than optimizing specific algorithms for specific properties (e.g., thermal vs. electrical properties behave differently).

            4.  METHODOLOGY


            2. Data Collection and Curation
We performed a systematic review of literature databases (Google Scholar, Scopus, ScienceDirect). A dataset of 400+ distinct ferroelectric ceramic compositions was curated from experimental literature.
            * Imputation Strategy: An Advanced KNN Imputer was utilized to fill missing target values based on chemical similarity (nearest neighbours in compositional space), maximizing data utility without introducing statistical bias.
Search Keywords: "KNN piezoelectric properties", "Lead-free ceramics d33", "Curie temperature machine learning."
Inclusion Criteria: Papers reporting chemical composition, sintering temperature, d33 and Tc for KNN-based ceramics.
Result: A dataset of 250+ unique compositions of d33 and Tc was compiled. 
Cautions: Those 250 datapoints contains some artificially generated datapoints for Tc for the initial testing of our platform. But these datasets can be easily changed anytime via our user interface, to get an accurate prediction later.

            3. Feature Engineering (Chemical Parsing)
Raw chemical strings cannot be processed by algorithms. So, a custom Hybrid Parsing Engine was developed to transform raw chemical text into machine-learnable vectors. This system integrates the chemparse library with a specialized Recursive Regular Expressions (Regex) layer to create a feature engineering pipeline:
            1. Parsing: The string is broken down into constituent elements and their stoichiometry.
            2. Vectorization/ Elemental Descriptors: A sparse matrix is created representing the Molar Fraction of elements (A-site: K, Na, Li, Bi...; B-site: Nb, Ta, Sb, Zr, Ti...). Currently supporting 24 Features, but it can be easily expanded by just adding the new element’s chemical symbol.
            3. Normalization: Input features were scaled to ensure model stability. Correctly parsing nested brackets (e.g., (K0.5Na0.5)) and varying stoichiometry notations.
            4. Physics-Based Descriptors: Weighted averages of fundamental atomic properties, ensuring the model "understands" the chemistry:
 Atomic Mass
 Atomic Radius (critical for tolerance factors)
 Electronegativity (bond character)
 Valence Electron Count
  

 
Fig 4.2.1: Workflow Diagram
               4. Machine Learning Architecture: Dual-Model Strategy
We implemented a Dual-Model Strategy, recognizing that electrical (d33) and thermal (Tc) properties have different underlying physical variances. The core engine provides users with two distinct pathways to train production models:
               1. Automatic Intelligent Model Tuning ("Auto-Mode")
               1. Logic: The system runs a comprehensive benchmark across multiple algorithms to find the global optimum.
               2. Model Zoo: Random Forest, XGBoost, LightGBM, Gradient Boosting, SVM (SVR), Kernel Ridge, and Gaussian Process.
               3. Selection Criteria: The system automatically compares the R2 score (Coefficient of Determination). It logically creates a Stacked Ensemble or selects the single best-performing model for each property (d33 and Tc) independently.
               2. Manual Custom Tuning
               1. Logic: Gives experts granular control to test specific hypotheses.
               2. Granular Control: Users can choose specific models (e.g., "Use XGBoost for d33 but SVR for Tc").
               3. Education: The UI includes "i-button" tooltips explaining complex hyperparameters (e.g., Learning Rate, Tree Depth, Epsilon) in plain English.

## 📖 Overview


This project leverages **Materials Informatics** and **Machine Learning** to accelerate the discovery of high-performance, lead-free piezoelectric materials.


By training advanced regression models on chemical composition data, this application provides a "Virtual Laboratory" that can predict critical material properties—specifically the **Piezoelectric Coefficient ($d_{33}$)** and **Curie Temperature ($T_c$)**—**in seconds, replacing months of traditional trial-and-error experimentation.**


---


## 🌍 The Problem: The "Lead Dilemma"


For decades, **Lead Zirconate Titanate (PZT)** has been the industry standard for piezoelectric devices (sensors, actuators, ultrasound) due to its exceptional performance. However, PZT creates a significant global challenge:


* **Toxicity:** PZT contains over 60% lead by weight, a neurotoxin that poses severe health risks.
* **Environmental Impact:** E-waste containing PZT leaches lead into soil and groundwater, causing long-term contamination.
* **Regulatory Pressure:** Global directives like **RoHS** are restricting the use of hazardous substances, creating an urgent need for eco-friendly alternatives.


Finding a lead-free replacement is difficult because the search space of possible chemical combinations is vast. Traditional "cook and look" methods are too slow and expensive to explore this space effectively.


## 💡 The Solution


This project implements a **Data-Driven Workflow** to bypass traditional limitations:


1.  **Data Collection:** Aggregated data on lead-free ceramics (specifically KNN-based) from scientific literature.
2.  **Feature Engineering:** Developed a robust chemical parser to convert complex chemical formulas (including solid solutions and dopants) into numerical features.
3.  **Predictive Modeling:** Trained and compared multiple ensemble learning algorithms (XGBoost, Random Forest, LightGBM) to accurately predict material properties.
4.  **Web Application:** Wrapped the ML engine in a modern Full-Stack application, allowing researchers to easily test new compositions and retrain models with new data.


---


## 🚀 Features


### 1. 🧪 Interactive Property Prediction
* **Smart Parsing:** Handles complex stoichiometry, solid solutions (e.g., `0.96(KNN)-0.04(BNT)`), and dopants.
* **Dual-Target Prediction:** Simultaneously predicts:
    * **$d_{33}$:** Piezoelectric charge coefficient (pC/N).
    * **$T_c$:** Curie Temperature (°C).
* **Formula Builder:** A GUI-based tool to construct valid chemical formulas without typing errors.


### 2. 📊 Automated Model Training & Fine-Tuning
* **Dynamic Retraining:** Upload new datasets (`.csv`) directly via the UI to retrain the models.
* **Hyperparameter Tuning:** Adjust `n_estimators`, `learning_rate`, and `max_depth` via simple sliders.
* **Real-time Visualization:** Instantly generates Research-Grade plots to validate the new model:
    * **Comparison Bar Charts:** Compares $R^2$ and RMSE across multiple algorithms.
    * **Regression Scatter Plots:** Visualizes "Predicted vs. Experimental" values with ideal fit lines.


### 3. 💾 Model Management
* **Production Deployment:** "One-click" deployment of the best-performing model to the production environment.
* **Consistency:** Ensures the exact parsing logic used during training is applied during prediction.
